DisasterM3 vs BRIGHT — Optical Data Audit & EDA

Ridita Afrin · CSE499B Group 5 First version: Oct 19, 2026 Easy English version, corrected: Oct 22, 2026

We checked two datasets, DisasterM3 and BRIGHT. We looked only at normal color photos. We left out radar (SAR) pictures.

We can use both datasets for our color-photo model. But we found two big things:

  1. DisasterM3 has a leak in its test set.
  2. Our five BRIGHT events can show the model where buildings are. They cannot show it what destroyed buildings look like.

The 7 main points

  1. There is a leak in the test set. We have 645 test pairs. 40 of them (6.2%) are almost copies of learning pairs. The reason: two xBD events, socal_fire and woolsey_fire, have the same 264 "before" photos. These 40 pairs hold 8.6% of all destroyed pixels in the test set.
  2. Our BRIGHT tiles do not leak. Our five events have 1,285 tiles. None of them is in the photos our model learns from or is tested on.
  3. BRIGHT has no color "after" photo. All 4,246 BRIGHT "after" pictures are SAR. So we get only "before" photos. In a "before" photo, every building still stands, so the tiles go in as intact. 82% of these tiles are volcano photos, but the test set has only 8 volcano pairs.
  4. The papers give the wrong resolution. BRIGHT's own files do not match BRIGHT's paper for 6 of 14 events. The DisasterM3 paper says 0.8 m, but its copies of BRIGHT tiles are 0.30 m.
  5. Our photos look different. Our five events are darker (pixel middle 60, pool 84) and 2.6 times sharper than the photos the model reads.
  6. "Before" and "after" photos do not line up. The paper says they match exactly. But in 52% of the pairs we could measure, the photos are moved by more than 2 pixels, and in a few pairs by up to 56 pixels.
  7. File names hide SAR. 73% of DisasterM3's SAR "after" files have no "sar" in the name. The tags and the pixels are right. Only the names are wrong.

How we find SAR (the SAR rule)

  • A color photo has 3 channels: red, green and blue.
  • In a SAR picture, the 3 channels are the same.
  • So: if the 3 channels are almost the same (the difference is less than 1 grey level), the picture is SAR.
  • We check only the pixels that have data. We skip black empty pixels.
  • We also compare our result with DisasterM3's tags (image_type, post_image_type). We need the pixel test because 2,066 files have no tag.

Pairs, the pool, and BRIGHT's photos

  • Pairs. The pairs come from the file train_release.json. It has 7,159 color pairs and 2,647 SAR pairs. They use 9,116 pre photos. fixed
  • The pool. Our champion model uses 6,443 color pairs. They are split with random_state=42: 5,798 for learning and 645 for testing. The 645 pairs are the frozen test set of our E-protocol.
  • BRIGHT has color photos only from before the disaster. Its folder is called pre-event_wo_ukraine_myanmar_mexico ("wo" means "without"). So it has no photos of Ukraine (513 tiles), Myanmar (126) and Mexico (212). These 3 events have labels and SAR, but no color photo here.

3Resolution and photo size

BRIGHT's own files say 0.2 to 0.6 m per pixel. DisasterM3 has 2,828 exact copies of BRIGHT tiles, and they are still 0.30 m, not the 0.8 m that the DisasterM3 paper says.

  • Beirut has the biggest gap: 0.50 m in the files, 1.0 m in the paper.
  • Noto is a little off: 0.47 m in the files, 0.50 m in the paper. We do not count it in the six. fixed
  • Tile size. Every color photo in both datasets is 1024 × 1024 pixels: 17,451 in DisasterM3 and 3,395 in BRIGHT.
  • DisasterM3's 0.8 m. The paper (§3) says every photo was changed to 0.8 m. But its 2,828 BRIGHT copies have a middle value of 0.30 m. DisasterM3 photos have no location or scale saved in them, so we cannot measure the rest.

4Brightness and color

Our five BRIGHT events look the most different from the photos the model learns from and is tested on. They are darker, they have less color, and they are sharper. Their difference from pool post photos is 0.11 bits (0 means the same, 1 means totally different).

Most of this gap comes from La Palma. Volcano ground is dark (its middle photo brightness is 60). But DisasterM3's own events already go from 61 (Hurricane Florence) to 130 (Maui), so dark photos are not new to the model.

Two ways to measure brightness. This report uses two numbers, and they are not the same thing: fixed

  • Pixel middle: 60 vs 84. We put all the pixels of a group together, then take the middle value.
  • Photo middle: 66 vs 89. We find each photo's average brightness first, then take the middle of those.
  • Pre vs post inside DisasterM3. If we put all pre photos together and all post photos together, they look almost the same (0.002 bits). But one pair at a time, they differ: the middle difference is 0.057 bits, and 18% of pairs differ by more than 0.2 bits. The reasons are flood water, burn marks, another satellite or another season.
  • Color. The middle color strength (saturation) is 0.30 for pool post photos and 0.21 for our five events. The photo middle brightness is 89 vs 66.
  • Black empty areas (no-data). 10.3% of pool photos and 16.2% of BRIGHT tiles (549 of 3,395) are more than 10% black. fixed The worst BRIGHT events are Bata and Goma (28% of their tiles each), Marshall (27%) and Derna (26%).
  • Cloud, haze and smoke. 139 pool post photos are more than 20% covered by bright, colorless areas (cloud, haze or smoke). Only 15 pre photos are. So bad weather on the disaster day hits the post photos most, and the model reads the post photos.

5Do pre and post photos line up?

In half of the pairs we could measure, the post photo is moved by more than 2 pixels from the pre photo (middle value 2.1 pixels). The paper says the pairs are "strictly aligned". That is not true. How far they move depends on the event.

  • By event. Portugal wildfire pairs are almost right (0.8 pixels). Hurricane Harvey is moved 7.1 pixels, Turkey scene 7 is moved 10.5 pixels and Morocco 18.7 pixels.
  • How many we could measure. Only 2,966 of 7,159 pairs (41%). In the other pairs, real change (broken buildings), cloud or black areas stop the two photos from matching. 47 pairs have no usable piece at all.
  • Worst cases. turkey_earthquake1 pairs 507, 516 and 522 are moved 40–56 pixels. hurricane_florence pairs 107, 150, 427 and 476 are moved 26–31 pixels.
  • Who is hurt. Our champion model reads only the post photo, so this problem does not hurt it. A model that reads both photos (Siamese or change detection) must line the photos up first, or learn with shifted copies.

6Labels: how many of each class

Where the model is tested, destroyed buildings are very few: only 3.6% of building pixels in the test set are destroyed, and 66.5% of test photos have no destroyed pixel at all.

BRIGHT's labels match its paper (14.1% building pixels; 82.7 / 10.7 / 6.6%). So the labels are fine. The problem is the photo: our pipeline sees only the "before" view, when every building still stands. Also, 80.9% of our tiles have no destroyed pixel, so the destroyed buildings sit in a few tiles only.

  • The merge is clean. Our notebooks join three masks (intact, damaged, destroyed) into one map. No pixel is in two masks, and every mask holds only 0 or 255. So the order of joining does not matter.
  • Big differences between events. Inside the pool, the destroyed share of building pixels goes from 0.07% (Turkey scene 0) to 63% (Maui).
  • Counting buildings. The pool has 249,623 buildings (middle size 1,030 pixels). BRIGHT's maps have 211,318, but its paper says 384,596. The reason: buildings that touch are counted as one. Example: in Turkey we count 66,055, the paper says 135,033.
  • Empty tiles. 839 BRIGHT tiles (19.8%) have no building at all. 315 of them are in our five events (24.5% of their tiles). No pool photo is empty.
  • Roads. Pool road pixels are 92.0% intact, 6.6% flooded and 1.4% covered by debris. Our segmentation model does not use roads.

10What to do

Fix the leak first. Adding our BRIGHT tiles is safe, but do not expect it to raise the Destroyed score.

  1. Report the score two times. Keep the score on all 645 test pairs, so we can compare with the old runs R1–R6. Also report the Destroyed IoU on the 605 clean pairs (the pairs not in val_train_leaks.csv). If we ever make a new split, group the pairs by the fingerprint (hash) of the pre photo, so twins always stay on the same side.
  2. Keep BRIGHT Turkey, Morocco and Libya out of training. 66 test pairs use these tiles as pre photos. Our five events are fine: none of their tiles is in the pool.
  3. Expect little change from our five events. They add "before" photos with buildings labeled intact. 82% of them are volcano, and the test set has only 8 volcano pairs. Their labels mark 21% of building pixels as destroyed, but there is no color "after" photo of these events on our computer. The only one anywhere is Noto: 68 photos in DisasterM3 Bench.
  4. Make the photos look alike before mixing. Our five events are darker (photo middle 66 vs 89), sharper, and their buildings are smaller (471 vs 1,030 pixels). fixed
    • Fix the brightness of each photo by itself (per-image normalization).
    • Add blur and color changes during learning (augmentation).
    • Think about making these photos 1.5 times bigger. The buildings are about 2.2 times smaller in area, and 1.5 × 1.5 = 2.25, so this makes the sizes match.
  5. Find SAR by tag or pixels, never by file name. Stop using the name-based SAR check in dm3_bright_audit.py and dm3_vs_bright_compare.py.
  6. Skip crops that are mostly black. 10–16% of tiles are more than 10% black. When we cut a random crop, we throw it away if more than half of it is black.
  7. Line up pairs before any pre+post model. The champion reads only post photos. But a Siamese model would see a middle shift of 2 pixels, and more than 5 pixels in 1 of every 10 pairs.
  8. Take resolution from the file tags, not from the papers. BRIGHT's Table 2 is wrong for 6 events. DisasterM3's BRIGHT copies are 0.30 m, not 0.8 m.

What the measures mean

  • High-frequency share (sharpness): how much fine detail is in the middle 512 × 512 pixels of a photo (the power between half and full Nyquist frequency). Blurry or stretched photos score near 0.
  • JS divergence (difference in bits): how different two brightness patterns are. 0 means the same, 1 means totally different.
  • Reliable shift: we cut both photos into pieces of 256 pixels and measure how far each piece moved (phase correlation). We trust the result only if at least 4 pieces give a clear signal (peak 0.1 or more) and agree within 1.5 pixels.
  • Building count (components): a group of building pixels that touch each other. Buildings that touch are counted as one, so busy areas get too few.

Context
The U-Net is the system's spatial cortex, and its weakest part
The hybrid pipeline answers building-counting questions from the U-Net's mask, builds zoomed collages from its boxes, and will draw survivability heatmaps from it. Every one of those inherits the mask's errors.
The improvement guide's rule of thumb: below 0.30 IoU for a class, counting that class from mask contours is unreliable. That is why the Phase Gate asks for Destroyed IoU above 0.30.

Context · Class imbalance
Imbalance shapes how we score and train
Accuracy is a trap
Calling every pixel background is 92.8% accurate and finds zero buildings. So we score each class by its own IoU, never by overall accuracy.
Destroyed is the hardest class
It is 3 pixels in every 1,000 and absent from 63% of images, so the model rarely sees an example. R1 and R6 score it 0.27–0.28 IoU, against 0.95 for background.
Every fix targets the imbalance
  • Focal Tversky: a missed rare pixel costs more
  • Weighted sampler: destroyed-building images drawn 10× as often
  • Copy-paste: extra damaged and destroyed buildings pasted into crops
Pixel share over all 6,443 images: background 92.77% · intact 5.86% · damaged 1.05% · destroyed 0.33% (dataset audit, A1).

Round 1 · The decision
Why we started an ablation study
Where we were in September
  • Baseline stuck at 0.46 mIoU
  • Destroyed IoU 0.17, below the 0.30 that counting from masks needs
  • Destroyed is 0.33% of pixels, about 280× rarer than background
  • The model scored well by mostly predicting background
Four suspected causes, from the improvement guide
A
Data. Most images contain no destroyed pixel, so most training steps give the model no signal for that class.
B
Architecture. Plain U-Net skip connections join mismatched encoder and decoder features, which can blur small, irregular rubble.
C
Loss. Weighted CE is swamped by background and unstable at about 280:1; Dice gives noisy gradients for tiny regions.
D
Resolution. Resizing 1024 → 512 discards 75% of pixels: an 8 × 8 shack becomes 4 × 4, indistinguishable from noise.
Why an ablation, not random tweaks: change one variable at a time so every gain has a known cause, run the four tracks in parallel on four Kaggle accounts, combine the winners on day 9, and check the Phase Gate on day 10.

Audit · The fix
One protocol, one variable, one noise bar
The E-protocol
The same 645 validation images at full native 1024 × 1024, padded to a multiple of 32. One dataset-level confusion matrix gives per-class IoU, precision, recall, F1 and F2. The last epoch is reported, so validation never picks the checkpoint.
One variable per run
R1 is the reference recipe. R2–R5 and R8 each change one thing relative to R1. E0 re-scores the old checkpoints with no training.
A measured noise bar
R6 repeats R1 with seed 7. A change counts only if it beats R1 by more than |R1 − R6| without lowering Destroyed IoU.
Built into Champion_Task/D4_champion_model.ipynb: it resumes across Kaggle sessions, keeps one Hugging Face repo per run, and writes results to D4_R1.csv, D4_R2.csv and so on.

Future work · Segmentation
Next steps for the segmentation stage
Phase 2: more destroyed examples
If R7 misses the gate: add xBD data mapped to our classes (no or minor damage → intact, major → damaged, destroyed → destroyed) and retrain.
Two-stage model
Stage 1 finds buildings; stage 2 grades damage only inside them. Destroyed rises from 0.33% of all pixels to about 4.6% of building pixels.
Recall-tuned threshold
Lower the Destroyed decision threshold at inference to trade precision for recall, and report Destroyed recall and F2. No retraining.
Cleaner masks for counting
Morphological post-processing removes tiny spurious regions before buildings are counted from mask contours.
Instance segmentation
Separate touching buildings into instances, so counts stop merging adjacent roofs into one.
Loss and data hygiene
Try boundary loss or hard-example mining, and generate the summary table from per-run outputs instead of typing values.

Q&A prep
Questions we expect
Why not train directly on mIoU?
mIoU comes from hard decisions and has no useful gradient. We train on smooth stand-ins (Focal Tversky, then Lovász-Softmax) and grade with mIoU.
How do you know a gain isn't seed luck?
R6 repeats R1 with another seed. A change counts only if it beats R1 by more than |R1 − R6| = 0.0028 mIoU.
R1 has lower recall than the baseline. Isn't that worse for rescue?
82% of the baseline's "destroyed" pixels were false alarms. R1 is twice as precise with a higher F2, and a lower threshold can raise recall without retraining.
Why did MA-Net collapse?
Under CE + Dice, with 93% background and a 5× higher learning rate, it predicted only background. It ranks below U-Net++, which R4 re-tests.
Thank you. Questions?
